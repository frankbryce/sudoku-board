Why DoDad Puzzles exists

From a homemade LEGO Sudoku board to a puzzle system built to grow

A bunch of parents complimented a Lego Sudoku set I made for my sons a few years ago. Inspired by this, I have now designed a more functional sudoku set that allows for more growth, including pencil marking, and jigsaw sudoku variants. A 4×4 board is the perfect size to get started with my 3 year old, and it’s also the perfect size to teach more advanced concepts like x-wings to my 10 year old.

There are always free options if you would like to participate with DoDads, but there are always ways to spend money to save your time as well. You should spend that quality time with your kiddos, instead.

I hope you enjoy using DoDad puzzles in your home. Please @ me on social media if you are enjoying it.

One 4×4 board, years of growth

Gentle enough for a 3-year-old. Deep enough for anyone.

Each workbook level adds a single new logic idea across 168 graded puzzles—turning abstract pencil marks into physical pegs kids can touch, move, and reason through together.

    First Logic & Singles

Quick Maker & Parent Questions

What 3D printer do I need to print the free 4×4 set?

The one-piece 4×4 board and flush carry tray are 145.6 × 145.6 mm, designed to print cleanly in White PLA / PLA Matte on a Bambu Lab A1 Mini (180 × 180 mm bed) or any larger FDM printer. No multi-color AMS is required—just touch up the raised top pads with paint markers!

How should I print the free PDF workbooks?

Print any workbook PDF at 100% / Actual Size on US Letter paper. Color printing looks great with the Blue, Red, Tan, and Green tiles, and each color also carries a distinct shape symbol so black-and-white prints work too.

Why are there center AND corner peg holes in every cell?

Real Sudoku solvers use two distinct types of pencil marks: center marks (“this cell must be one of these colors”) and corner / Snyder marks (“this color goes in one of these cells inside this box”). Dedicated sockets make advanced logic visual and hands-on for kids.
